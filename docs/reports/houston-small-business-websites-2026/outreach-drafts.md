## LinkedIn post 1 (launch, personal voice)

I checked 1,178 Houston small business websites so you don't have to.

Over two weeks in September my outreach system audited every auto shop, home-service company, salon, and med spa it could find on Google Maps across the Heights, Spring Branch, Montrose, Downtown, and northwest Houston. Same checks on every one: does it have a site, does it load, is it secure, does it work on a phone, and what does Google score its speed.

What came back:

- 1 in 3 has no website of its own. Auto shops are highest at 37%.
- 1 in 12 listed websites is dead or shows a security warning. These businesses are sending customers from Google to a page that no longer exists.
- 27% of the sites that work fail Google's mobile speed test. Only 9% score "good."
- Not one salon or med spa site in the sample reached a 90.
- Half of all the sites we could evaluate have at least one serious problem.

The one that surprised me: 62 of the businesses with no website at all have 50+ reviews at 4.5 stars or better. They are doing fine. The listing is doing the work. The website question for them is not "do I need one" but "what happens when someone wants to know my prices at 9 pm."

## LinkedIn post 2 (the dead-links angle, two days later)

Click the website link on your own Google Business Profile today.

I mean it. Of 786 Houston businesses whose Google listing points to a real domain, 53 link to a site that is gone. The domain expired, the page 404s, or the host is down. Another 12 load behind an expired security certificate, so the customer gets a full-page "your connection is not private" warning instead of your homepage.

That is 1 in 12. Most of the owners have no idea, because nobody clicks their own link.

Sixty seconds, on your phone. If it is broken, the fix is usually a renewed domain or a certificate, not a new site.
